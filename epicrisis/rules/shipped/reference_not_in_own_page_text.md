# What it looks at

The same, number by number, against the model's own transcription of a scanned page. What it can
say is that the reading stored a range it did not write down in the text, and what it cannot say is
whether the form printed one.

# How it can be wrong

Ranges are the part of a form a transcription is likeliest to summarise rather than copy: a column
of norms standing beside a long table is the first thing a model shortens when it is writing prose.
So on archives of scans this fires for reasons that are about the shape of the transcription rather
than about the page.

Turning it off on such an archive is a reasonable thing to do, and the cost is named: a range
really invented by the reading would then be caught by nothing until somebody looked at the scan.
