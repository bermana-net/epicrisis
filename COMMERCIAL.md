## What a commercial licence is for

The licence text opens the door. What an organisation actually buys with it:

- the right to run the program in production, for its own people and its own clients;
- a data processing agreement under Article 28 GDPR, and answers to a security review;
- releases, and a named person to write to when something is wrong;
- terms of liability that a business can sign — the free version is provided as is, with the
  warranties disclaimed, and that is the honest position for something given away.

## What it is not

This program stores and displays. It does not interpret: it never says whether a value is normal,
never compares a value with anything but the reference range printed on its own form, never
diagnoses and never advises. It is **not a medical device**, and a commercial licence does not
turn it into one — an organisation putting it anywhere near a decision about a person's care is
responsible for what its own product does under the rules that apply to that product.

## Four years

Every version published under this licence becomes Apache 2.0 four years after it is published.
That is part of the licence, not a promise: whatever happens to this project or to the person who
wrote it, the code of 2026 is open source in 2030.
